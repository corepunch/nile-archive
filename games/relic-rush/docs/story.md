# Relic Rush: A Light Below

**Status:** Fresh campaign plan and playable first chapter. Chapters 2–3 have provisional bridge dialogue so existing level slots remain coherent; they need a full dialogue pass and cold read. The town tracks quests and decoration points. Chapter 1 uses the existing character portraits and island views.

**Play fantasy:** Rebuild an expedition base and follow a light answering from beneath the island. The feeling is curious, warm-hearted pulp adventure: danger gives friends a reason to act for one another.

## What is broken

The camp is abandoned, the well is fouled, old paths are overgrown, and an unattended tide relay sends a false all-clear. Its lamp is useful to boats but can draw them towards a damaged quay. Each board's obstacles are dressed as the problem the crew is solving there (see [Board dressing](#board-dressing-obstacles-as-the-scene)). Restorations give the team places to work and meet.

## Truth timeline

1. Years ago island keeper Ines maintained a tide-room relay to warn boats away from the lower quay when its landing was unsafe. The chart behind the well pump records the relay route; the lamp bracket at camp lets a lookout answer.
2. Tomas and Ines worked together. When the quay wall cracked, Tomas closed the room; Ines left to fetch repair plans and supplies. Tomas promised to maintain the warning, but a storm cut the cable. He believes Ines did not return.
3. Recently Ines returned by a different shore. She found the room flooded and, unable to reach camp, used its hand shutter to send the old warning. She changed its rhythm to attract attention. Tomas does not know she is back.
4. Adrian financed the expedition using a promised exclusive claim on island discoveries. He has not told Elena; the claim puts the relay and islanders' work at risk. Kessler represents the claim holder, not a supernatural threat.
5. The team repairs the route and relay, finds Ines's signed repair note and learns the warning's purpose. Adrian gives up the claim, and the crew makes the quay safe. When Mara tests the warning lamp, Ines waits and answers with the same three flashes, from the safe landing. Tomas gives her the key, and she brings a chart of the other stations.

## Board dressing: obstacles as the scene

**Status:** Story materials are in play across the campaign. The later chapters still use provisional dialogue.

A board is not a pause between scenes. It is the crew doing the task the last line named: cutting creepers off the path, gathering pieces of the tide chart, prising barnacles from the shutter. The rules stay as they are. Each rule family can wear several looks, and a board picks the look its task calls for.

### Rule families and the readability contract

| Family | Rule (unchanged) | Base look, taught first | Story looks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lock** | Holds a piece in place; one hit per strand (`chain`, `chain-2`) | Iron chain | Creeper vine, rope lashing, cable snarl, claim seal |
| **Cover** | Encases a piece; one hit per layer (`ice-1`, `ice-2`) | Salt crust | Barnacle crust, wall rubble, ice (frozen boards only) |
| **Floor** | Match on spreading silt or mud. Match through cleared stone to sweep drift sand. | Stonework | Well mud, river silt, drift sand |

Each family keeps one visual language in every look, so a player who knows the rule recognises it at once:

- **Lock** looks span the hex between two bolted edges like the chain does, and the two-hit state crosses two strands.
- **Cover** looks trace the whole face in the intact state and lose the upper edge after a hit. Tropical covers have an opening so the piece inside stays readable.
- **Floor** looks sit flat under the pieces with countable cell joints.
- **HUD and lessons.** The goal icon and counters use the board's look, and the lesson text names it.

The [asset catalogue](asset-catalogue.md#story-materials) records every painted state and its runtime mapping.

**Teach on the base look, then dress it.** A rule first appears in its base look, with the lesson text. The board that brings in a new look has one crew line tying it to the rule the player already knows ("same as the pump chains"). After that the look needs no explanation.

### Flow around a board

1. **The scene line names the job.** The last line before Play names what is in the way ("The pump chain has rusted fast. Break it and gather the loose chart pieces.").
2. **The board shows that job.** The board's skin and lesson text match the line.
3. **The item card hands the clue to the story.** The first-clear exchange can refer to the recovered object once players have seen it.
