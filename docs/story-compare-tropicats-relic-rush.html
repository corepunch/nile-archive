Tropicats vs Relic Rush: Landfall

Both games are a match-3 where the player rebuilds a place and a few characters react to it. The difference is what a building means. In Tropicats every repair is a small reveal about someone you already know. In Landfall a building mostly announces what it is for.

10,715 lines · 1,689 scenesTropicats, 70 chapters, 2,730 levels. About 3.9 lines and 0.6 scenes per level. 25+ recurring speakers; the top two (Ava, Elsie) have ~1,070 lines each.
224 lines · 129 scenesRelic Rush, 100 levels. 103 lines in 42 campaign scenes, the rest town talk. About 2.2 lines per level. 3 speakers: Sela 82, Oren 73, Tavi 69.
What fires a sceneTropicats: 1,080 on task completed (a repair), 297 quest start, 273 claim, only 33 on a level clear. Landfall: first clear of listed levels, plus building placement.

Opening hook Earthquake. Elsie asks the Chief to stand the Great Totem back up. First line is a joke and a cheer: one scene per tiny repair (rubble, square, totem).

A request, then the work Each 10-level quest opens with a character saying what they need and why now. 3 to 5 repair tasks follow. Cubby wants to find the totem-cutter but is hungry, so: fix the grill.
chp3_q2 · 297 quest starts		 Request lines are spread thin: Sela asks for a workshop, Tavi for a lodge. The need is sometimes a sentence about the town ("a town needs more than a dozen people") instead of one person's problem. Make every purchase someone's own errand, said in their voice, with a reason that's about them (Oren's soup got rained on: already good). Aim for one named person per building.
Repair reveals a person The strongest trick. The thing just fixed tells you something private. The grill: Cubby's old love letters to Ava, never sent. A bench is where he naps. A coral he once gave Ava ended up back on the beach. The surfboard is his father's; he claims a seaweed allergy to avoid water.
chp3_q2.1–4, chp3_q4.1–3		 Building scenes caption function: "A roof that doesn't flap in the wind." Funny, but it could be about any roof. Exceptions work: Sela's cup, Tavi on the beached ship ("built in Callira"). Per building, one object-and-memory line: what Oren left at home, whose boat the old steps were, Tavi's mother and the reef. The rule says say something the screen doesn't; this is how.
Running personal want Three crushes drive 30+ chapters: Cubby loves Ava, Ava rejects him, Olivia loves Cubby, he asks Olivia out. Each repair moves it a step, never resolves in one scene. Plus a love-rival jealousy beat (Ava "can't believe he fell for that nerd").
chp3_q2, chp4_q1, chp5_q1		 Oren and bread, Sela and pots, Tavi and her mate/ship. These are jobs, not hopes. Nobody wants something from another person. Give each of the three one small person-to-person want that keeps ticking (Tavi respects the reef, wants Sela to learn charts; Oren wants Tavi to admit she likes his bread). Warm, no tragedy, fits our tone rules.
Running gags Kip and his pet crab; Cubby's belly and bananas ("42 grilled bananas"); the shaman's nonsense wisdom; Ava's constant grudges. A gag recurs in other scenes so it pays off. Sela: "Everybody wants pots." Oren: bread. Good gags, each used four to six times, but mostly in a different scene each. Tie one gag to an object the player placed, and bring it back across scenes. Oren's soup that got rained on can return when the roof leaks again.
Quest-end turn The claim scene ends on a reveal that creates the next quest. "The totem was cut down." "The suspect is a dog." The tea party becomes a trap, so the next quest is repairing Lily's trust.
ftchp2_q1, ftchp4_q3, ftchp5_q2		 Mostly bridges: "Now we need roofs before the rain finds us." One real hook: the old steps, "Where did they all go?", but it answers itself with no payoff. End each four-building epoch on a question or a small surprise, not a to-do. Use the steps and the Callira stones: one discovery per epoch that changes how the cast sees the place.
Disagreement Friction is constant but harmless: Ava wants to trap the dog, Lily protects him, Elsie keeps saying "shush". Same cast, different positions; resolved by an errand for the player.
chp5_q2–q3		 Almost none. One good case: the Breadhaven vote ("That's not a vote, Oren. That's a bribe"). One recurring friendly argument per epoch (Sela vs Tavi on the old stones: dig or leave). Our tone rule is no villains, not no disagreements.
New people A new named character arrives with their own building to repair: Olivia's study, the shaman's shack, Lily's hut. They stay for the rest of the story. About a new face every 4 chapters.
chp4_q2, chp5_q4		 Three characters for 100 levels. Hill people and the goat girl are mentioned but have no face or line. The families land at the end as "fifty-two people". Add 2 to 3 named residents, each introduced by the building that houses them: the goat girl (trading post), a hill elder (market), Tavi's mate (lighthouse, who leaves). The player meets them as a reward.
Tutorial and play voice Characters address the Chief by name, a mix of in-joke and gentle teaching ("remind you how to build"). Scenes fire for the task, not the board, so the player feels the connection between a clear and a fix. Addresses "builder". Level lessons live in the lesson line, not in the story, which is by our rule. Keep. Name the player once or twice at emotional spikes (thanks, teasing).
Ending Chapter 70 ends with a farewell visit to every friend; the Chief takes the crown. The last quest is a walk around the cast.
chp70_q4		 Families land, fountain, the vote. Strong closer: Sela leaves the cup, the town takes the old name, Callira. Add Tropicats' farewell walk: visit each named person once more (levels 97 to 99) before the vote. Cheap, and it is what players remember.

Short list: what to steal first

  1. Object-and-memory line in every building scene. One personal fact attached to the thing just built. This is the biggest gap and costs nothing in art.
  2. Give each building an owner. The owner opens, a second person reacts. Settles who speaks and makes the town a set of homes.
  3. Three to four more named residents, each arriving with a building. The goat girl, a hill elder, Tavi's mate. Introduce at the first build and keep one line per epoch for them.
  4. One person-to-person want per main character that advances by a step per epoch. Tropicats' love triangle is the model; ours should be friendly (Sela wants Tavi's chart lessons, Oren wants Tavi to compliment the bread).
  5. End each epoch on a reveal, not a to-do. The old steps and Callira stones are the supply.
  6. A farewell walk at levels 97 to 99.
